NOTES
================================================================================
- The soak test design is rigorous and comprehensive. Key strengths:
  * [P1] uses 12,000 iid N(0,1) ticks with 3 axes — well above the 10^4
    minimum. The mean D2 check at [0.95A, 1.05A] provides a tight band
    around the expected chi-square mean (E[D2] = A for H0).
  * [P2] checks ALL result fields (D2, p_value, S_A, theta, sigma_S,
    deviation, thresholds, S_vector), not just the top-level flags. The
    adversarial patterns cover zeros, extreme magnitudes, mixed signs,
    tiny values, constants, and zero-variance axes.
  * [P3] uses vectorized batch scoring (1000-candidate chunks) with
    chi2.sf oracle validation.
  * [R1] has clearly delineated phases with phase-specific threshold
    assertions, not just a single end-to-end check.

- The lifecycle test's spike phase (x_a=20.0) produces detectable anomalies
  because by tick 110 the joint detector has accumulated ~110 S-vector samples
  with mean near 0 (from steady state) or slightly positive (from drift).
  The 20σ spike on a single axis creates a large Mahalanobis distance.

- Edge cases covered: all-constant metrics, zero-variance axes, population
  scoring with extreme candidates (±1e6), warm-up gating in both Tier 1
  (min_warm) and Tier 2 (min_population).

- Test isolation: all soak tests create fresh states per test, no shared
  mutable state. The Monte Carlo tests use fixed seeds for reproducibility.
